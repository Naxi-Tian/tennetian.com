Scent sensor + ML classifier

A seven-week nanotechnology project combining gas-sensing hardware with a classification pipeline for food-allergen scent data.

RoleTeam lead
ProgramUPenn ESAP Nanotechnology
Team3 members

The concept

Can a wearable learn the signature of a scent?

We explored how a compact gas-sensing system could turn raw, unlabeled measurements into a useful prediction about the presence of food allergens such as peanut and gluten.

My work

Leading across sensor design and model training.

  • Led a three-member team building the wearable sensor system.
  • Organized raw scent-sensor data for collection and labeling.
  • Trained a machine-learning classifier on the measured signals.
  • Used microfluidics, lithography, and band-gap theory to inform sensor design choices.
Scent sample→Sensor response→Dataset→ML classifier→Allergen class

What it connected

Nanoscale physics to a prediction pipeline.

The project became an early model for how I now approach engineering: understand the sensing material, design a reliable acquisition path, and shape the resulting data into an interpretable software output.
