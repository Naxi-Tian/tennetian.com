Graphene pollen biosensor

A chemically modified electrochemical sensor designed to detect airborne pollen allergens with high sensitivity and specificity.

RoleResearcher
InstitutionChinese Academy of Sciences
RecognitionRegeneron ISEF 2026 Grand Award

The question

Can an invisible allergen become an electrical signal?

Airborne pollen is easy to count but harder to characterize by the proteins that actually trigger allergic reactions. This project explored graphene as a highly conductive substrate whose electrical behavior changes when target allergens bind to a chemically modified surface.

What I did

From material preparation to field validation.

  • Fabricated, chemically modified, and characterized graphene-based electrochemical sensors.
  • Measured resistance and electrochemical response to identify allergen binding.
  • Validated experimental results against hospital and weather-station field data.
  • Documented methodology and iteration in a 40,000-word experimental log.
Graphene→Surface chemistry→Allergen binding→Electrical response→Validation

Outcome

A research result that became a deployed system.

The research was published in Advances in Materials Physics and Chemistry, earned a Regeneron ISEF 2026 Grand Award in Biomedical Engineering, and became the sensing foundation for four live monitoring stations in Beijing.
